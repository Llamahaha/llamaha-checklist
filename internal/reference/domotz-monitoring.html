Domotz Monitoring

Operational checklist for using Domotz to monitor networks, devices, alerts, and site health without exposing client-specific settings.

Overview

Purpose and common use cases

Purpose

Domotz is used to discover monitored network devices, track availability, review alert history, and give technicians a fast view of what changed at a site before deeper troubleshooting starts.

Common use cases

  • Confirm whether an outage is isolated to one endpoint, one switch, one ISP circuit, or a whole site.
  • Review device uptime, response, packet loss, and alert history before calling a site contact.
  • Validate newly onboarded network equipment is visible and labeled correctly.
  • Spot unknown devices, changed IPs, offline infrastructure, or recurring alerts that need cleanup.

Onboarding

Checklist for a new site or device group

Site setup

  • Confirm the site name, location label, primary contact, timezone, and maintenance window placeholders.
  • Verify the Domotz agent location and document whether it is appliance-based, software-based, or hosted on network equipment.
  • Record non-secret settings such as monitored subnet ranges, VLAN labels, critical device names, and expected ISP handoff notes.
  • Do not store passwords, license keys, private tunnel URLs, or tenant identifiers in the guide or ticket notes.

Device tagging

  • Label firewalls, switches, wireless controllers, access points, servers, NAS devices, UPS units, printers, and carrier equipment.
  • Mark devices that should alert when offline. Avoid alerting on transient phones, guest devices, or personal endpoints unless there is a specific reason.
  • Use placeholders for MSP-specific alert policy names such as <CRITICAL_DEVICE_POLICY> and <SITE_MAINTENANCE_POLICY>.

Fast Checks

Device and site health triage

Before assuming outage

  • Check whether the Domotz agent itself is online. If the agent is offline, device-level alert data may be stale.
  • Compare current alerts with the alert timeline. Look for a single first-down device that explains downstream alerts.
  • Confirm whether the site is inside a documented maintenance window or power event.
  • Review whether the affected device changed IP, MAC address, switch port, or hostname recently.

Device checks

  • Open the device record and capture status, last seen time, IP, MAC, vendor, and linked alerts.
  • Check response time and packet loss trends for intermittent issues.
  • For network gear, compare uplink/downlink neighbors and whether multiple devices behind it dropped together.
  • If supported, review SNMP or TCP sensor status, but confirm sensor credentials and community strings are stored only in approved secret storage.

Alert Review

How to work an alert

Alert process

  1. Identify whether the alert is device, network, sensor, or agent related.
  2. Check the first alert time and any related devices that alerted within the same window.
  3. Confirm whether there is a known change, outage, power issue, maintenance window, or ISP ticket.
  4. Capture status evidence before clearing, suppressing, or changing alert rules.
  5. Update the ticket with impact, scope, next action, and whether monitoring has recovered.

Escalation notes

Escalate when the site agent is unreachable, multiple infrastructure devices dropped at once, recurring packet loss affects production traffic, a firewall or switch is unreachable, or alert policy changes are needed beyond normal support scope.

What To Gather Before Escalation

Ticket evidence

Collect

  • Site name placeholder, device name, device type, IP, MAC, and last seen time.
  • Alert title, first alert time, recovery time if any, and screenshot of the alert timeline.
  • Whether the Domotz agent is online and whether other critical devices are affected.
  • Any known power, ISP, firewall, switch, or maintenance event.
  • What user impact has been confirmed and who confirmed it.
